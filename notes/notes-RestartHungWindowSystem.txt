Unfortunately, SysRq doesn't offer that level of selectivity — it's a blunt instrument by design. The signal keys (E and I) target all processes except PID 1, with no way to exclude specific services like NetworkManager or systemd-resolved.
Your real tool here is loginctl or systemctl to kill just the session:
bash

loginctl kill-session self

or, more surgically, just kill the display manager session by targeting your current seat:
bash

loginctl terminate-seat seat0

This tears down everything attached to the graphical seat — your
compositor, Wayland/X session, all GUI apps — while leaving system
services like NetworkManager and resolved completely
untouched. Systemd then restarts GDM automatically.

If you want even finer control, you can kill just the compositor itself:

# For GNOME/Wayland
pkill -x gnome-shell

# For X11
pkill -x Xorg

GDM will notice the session died and respawn a fresh login screen.
The catch with all of these is that they require a working terminal —
either a TTY (Ctrl+Alt+F3) or SSH. If your window system is so stuck
you can't get to a terminal, then loginctl terminate-seat seat0 typed
blind into a virtual console (switch with Ctrl+Alt+F2, log in, run the
command) is your cleanest option before resorting to SysRq.

The sequence to remember is:

Ctrl+Alt+F2 (or F3) to switch to a TTY
Log in
loginctl terminate-seat seat0
Ctrl+Alt+F1 to switch back to the graphical seat once GDM restarts

It's a much cleaner solution than SysRq for a hung window system
specifically. And if the TTY itself is unresponsive (which can happen
if the GPU driver is truly wedged), then R followed by loginctl
terminate-seat seat0 typed blind is your fallback — at least you won't
nuke the network that way.
